What Credit Repair Companies Can and Cannot Do

Understanding the scope of services offered by credit repair companies is essential to setting realistic expectations. These firms operate within specific legal frameworks, primarily the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), which dictates what they can and cannot do.

What They Can Do:

  • Dispute Inaccurate Information: Their primary function is to challenge errors, outdated information, or unverifiable items on your credit reports with the three major credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion).
  • Send Dispute Letters: They prepare and send formal dispute letters on your behalf, citing relevant consumer protection laws.
  • Negotiate with Creditors: Some companies may assist in negotiating with creditors or collection agencies to remove negative items, though this is less common than direct disputes.
  • Provide Credit Education: Many offer resources and guidance on how to maintain good credit habits and understand your credit report.
  • Monitor Credit Reports: Many services include credit monitoring to track changes and new inaccuracies.

What They Cannot Do:

  • Remove Accurate Information: They cannot legally remove accurate, timely, and verifiable negative information from your credit report.
  • Create a New Credit Identity: Any company promising to create a new credit identity for you is engaging in illegal practices.
  • Guarantee Specific Results: While they can promise to perform services, they cannot guarantee a specific outcome or credit score increase, as results depend on the credit bureaus' investigations.
  • Charge Upfront Fees: Under CROA, credit repair organizations cannot charge for services until they have been performed.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: How long does credit repair take?

A: The duration of credit repair varies depending on the number and complexity of negative items on your report. It can range from a few months to over a year. Most companies report seeing initial results within 30-90 days.

Q: Can credit repair companies remove bankruptcies?

A: Credit repair companies cannot remove accurate bankruptcies from your credit report. Bankruptcies typically remain on your report for 7 to 10 years, depending on the type. They can only dispute inaccuracies related to the bankruptcy filing.

Q: Is it safe to use a credit repair company?

A: Yes, using a reputable credit repair company is safe. Look for companies that comply with the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), have positive reviews, and transparent pricing. Be wary of companies that promise unrealistic results or demand upfront payment.

Q: How much does credit repair cost?

A: The cost of credit repair varies widely among companies and service tiers. You can expect to pay a monthly fee ranging from $60 to $150, often with an initial setup fee. Some companies also offer a one-time flat fee.

Q: What should I look for in a credit repair company?

A: Key factors to consider include transparent pricing, a clear understanding of their dispute process, positive customer reviews, a good Better Business Bureau (BBB) rating, and a money-back guarantee. Ensure they do not make illegal promises, such as removing accurate information.

Q: Will credit repair hurt my credit score?

A: No, legitimate credit repair should not hurt your credit score. Its purpose is to improve your score by removing inaccurate or unverifiable negative items. If a company suggests actions that could harm your score, it's a red flag.

Focus on the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), which includes both interest and fees. Compare minimum credit score requirements, funding speed, loan amounts, and repayment terms. Read recent customer reviews on Trustpilot and the BBB. Getting pre-qualified lets you see real personalized offers.

The interest rate is the base cost of borrowing. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) includes the interest rate plus all fees (origination fees, closing costs, etc.), expressed as a yearly rate. APR gives you a more complete picture of the true cost of a loan — always compare APRs, not just interest rates.

Credit scores have a dramatic impact on rates. On a $20,000 personal loan, the difference between a 720 score (8% APR) and a 580 score (25% APR) is over $9,000 in additional interest over 5 years. Improving your score before applying can save thousands.
